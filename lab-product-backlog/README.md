# Product Backlog Lab

**Author:** Omar Mouttalie

## Objective

Understand what a Product Backlog is, what role it plays in Scrum, and build an initial backlog for a real-world product.

## Definition

The **Product Backlog** is an **ordered list** of everything needed to build or improve a product: features, bug fixes, improvements and technical work. It is owned and prioritized by the **Product Owner** and is the **single source of truth** for the work to be done. It is never finished and evolves with the product.

## Role

- **Captures** the needs of users, clients and stakeholders.
- **Prioritizes** work by value, risk and effort.
- **Feeds** the Sprint Backlog: the team pulls the top items each sprint.
- **Aligns** the team and stakeholders around one shared vision.
- **Adapts** to change through continuous refinement.

## Characteristics of a Good Backlog (DEEP)

| Letter | Meaning | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| D | Detailed appropriately | Top items are precise, lower ones stay rough |
| E | Emergent | Evolves with feedback and learning |
| E | Estimated | Each item has an effort estimate |
| P | Prioritized | Most valuable items come first |

## Backlog Item Format

Items are written as **user stories**:

> As a **[user]**, I want **[goal]**, so that **[benefit]**.

Each item should have an **acceptance criteria**, a **priority** and an **estimate**.

## Example: Food Delivery App

**Actors:** Customer, Restaurant, Driver, Administrator.

| Priority | User Story | Points |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | As a customer, I want to browse restaurants and menus. | 5 |
| P2 | As a customer, I want to place and pay for an order. | 8 |
| P3 | As a driver, I want to receive delivery requests. | 8 |
| P4 | As a customer, I want to track my order live. | 5 |
| P5 | As a customer, I want to rate a restaurant. | 3 |

## Without vs. With a Product Backlog

| Without | With |
|---|---|
| Lost requirements | One central, visible list |
| No clear priorities | Highest value delivered first |
| Scope creep | Controlled, refined changes |
| Team and stakeholders misaligned | Shared understanding |

## Lab Steps

1. Choose a product and identify its **actors**.
2. List their **needs and pain points**.
3. Write each need as a **user story**.
4. Add **acceptance criteria** and **estimates**.
5. **Prioritize** the items (P1, P2, ...).
6. Review the backlog with users and refine it.

## Conclusion

The Product Backlog turns scattered needs into a prioritized, actionable plan, reducing waste and increasing the value delivered to users.
