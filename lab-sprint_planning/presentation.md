# Sprint Planning

### The starting point of every Sprint in Scrum

**Presented by:** Omar Mouttalie
**Supervisor:** ES-SARRAJ Fouad  



---

## Introduction

<!-- notes:
 Hello everyone, my name is Omar Mouttalie, and today I will present Sprint Planning in the Scrum methodology. Sprint Planning is the event that starts every Sprint. By the end of this presentation, you will understand what it is, why it matters and how to do it well.
  -->

- Scrum delivers a working product in short cycles called **Sprints** (1 to 4 weeks).
- Every Sprint must start with a **shared plan**, otherwise the team works without direction.
- **Sprint Planning** is the event that creates this plan and kicks off the Sprint.

---

## Definition

<!-- notes:
 Sprint Planning is a Scrum event where the whole team decides what can be delivered in the Sprint and how the work will be done. It involves the Product Owner, the Scrum Master and the Developers, and it lasts a maximum of 8 hours for a one-month Sprint. It starts from a refined Product Backlog and produces two outputs: the Sprint Goal and the Sprint Backlog.
  -->


> **Sprint Planning** is a Scrum event where the whole Scrum Team decides **what** can be delivered in the Sprint and **how** the work will be done.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Participants** | Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers |
| **Timebox** | Max 8 hours for a one-month Sprint |
| **Input** | Refined Product Backlog, team capacity |
| **Output** | **Sprint Goal** and **Sprint Backlog** |

---

## Outline

<!-- notes:
 The meeting answers three questions, starting with why the Sprint is valuable, which gives us the Sprint Goal. Then we decide what can be done by selecting items from the Product Backlog, and how it will be done by breaking those items into tasks. After that, we will look at the common problems and their solutions.
  -->

1. **Why** is this Sprint valuable? (Sprint Goal)
2. **What** can be done? (select Product Backlog items)
3. **How** will it be done? (break items into tasks)
4. Problematic: common planning failures
5. Solutions: good practices
6. Conclusion

---

## Problematic

<!-- notes:
 Sprint Planning often fails when the team overcommits and plans more work than it can finish. Other problems are having no clear Sprint Goal, using vague backlog items, or working with a Product Owner who is absent. Changes during the Sprint make things worse, because the scope keeps growing and the plan collapses.
  -->

- **Overcommitment:** the team plans more than it can deliver.
- **No clear Sprint Goal:** work becomes a random list of tasks.
- **Unclear backlog items:** vague stories cause wrong estimates.
- **Absent Product Owner:** questions stay unanswered.
- **Mid-Sprint changes:** scope grows and the Sprint fails.

---

## Solutions

<!-- notes:
 First, the team should define a clear Sprint Goal to give the Sprint a purpose. Then, the Product Backlog must be refined before the meeting, and the team should plan according to its real capacity and past velocity. Finally, the whole team, including the Product Owner, must take part, and everyone should respect the timebox and protect the Sprint scope.
  -->

- Define a **Sprint Goal** first to give the Sprint a purpose.
- Keep the Product Backlog **refined** before the meeting.
- Plan using real **capacity and past velocity**.
- Involve the **whole team** and the Product Owner.
- **Respect the timebox** and protect the Sprint scope.

---

## Conclusion

<!-- notes:
 Sprint Planning turns the Product Backlog into an achievable plan for the Sprint. A clear Sprint Goal and a realistic Sprint Backlog keep the team focused and make delivery predictable. Thank you for your attention, and I am happy to answer your questions.
  -->


- Sprint Planning turns the **Product Backlog** into an **achievable plan**.
- A clear **Sprint Goal** and a realistic **Sprint Backlog** keep the team focused.
- Good planning means **predictable delivery** and **more value** every Sprint.

**Thank you for your attention!**
